What this is

A measurement program, not a leaderboard

The STAMP Protocol measures whether a score given to an AI model survives being taken twice. Every rate it publishes carries its interval and its conditions — which grader, which serving provider, which temperature, which bank version, how many draws — and every figure is registered with the SHA-256 of the files it was computed from. It publishes its own errors as findings. It is published by Budday Budderson Studio LLC, a New Mexico company, on alternate Saturdays at 15:37 America/Chicago, with working notes on The Bench on Sundays and Wednesdays.

It is not an audit, a certification or a regulatory judgment, and it does not measure helpfulness, harmlessness or honesty. It is also not the IETF network-measurement protocol of the same name, nor several other unrelated STAMPs — see Other STAMPs. The current issue is The Calibration Vol 001.

The design

Why the site looks the way it does

Every Budday Budderson site carries a colophon — the page a printed book keeps at the back to say how it was made. Ours explains the mark and why it reads as an H, the three typefaces, the red that means “a measured figure”, the seven finishes that change the site’s color with the day (and a playground where you can try all seven), why everything ships on a Saturday at 15:37, and how to check that what you are reading is what was issued.

Our own receipts

How to check any of this

Every issue’s HTML is byte-frozen and the first twelve characters of its SHA-256 are printed in the foot of every page of its PDF; the archive links the source file so you can hash it yourself. figures.json holds every citable figure with the fingerprints of its sources. An issued Calibration is never edited; corrections are findings and are published like findings.
