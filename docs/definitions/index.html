A reader should never have to guess what a word on these pages means. These are the terms as the program uses them; where a term has a wider meaning elsewhere, the narrower one here governs.

Measures

Rates and their conditions

TermMeaning here
Failure rateThe share of draws that failed their item’s written pass criterion. On this bank, which is adversarial by construction, it is not a general error rate and cannot be converted into one.
DrawOne response from one model to one probe under recorded conditions. Each candidate gets two halves of five draws per item.
On-bank intervalThe narrow 95% interval: this model, on these 63 items, draws random. Use it for week-on-week comparison.
Beyond-bank intervalThe wide 95% interval: this model, on probes like these, item choice itself uncertain. Use it for any claim about the model.
Noise floorThe gap between a candidate’s two halves — what the instrument produces when nothing changed. No movement smaller than it is reported.
ReliabilitySpearman-Brown full-test reliability from the two halves. Vol 001: 0.945.
Design effectHow many draws it takes to carry the information of one independent one, because most items sit at 0% or 100%. Vol 001: 616 draws ≈ 87 independent.
Agreement (AC1)Gwet’s AC1 across the three judges on the panel sample. Pre-registered floor 0.50; below it, nothing is published. Agreement is not correctness.
LeniencyHow far a grader sits from the panel mean on identical draws. Reported, and corrected for, never assumed.
AttritionDraws lost to truncation, refusal or unparseable output. Above 5%, a run is disqualified, because what vanishes is never random.

Grades

How a finding is labeled

  • Publishable — the evidence clears the pre-registered gates and the noise floor.
  • Provisional — direction not in doubt, but a condition is unmet (a half unfinished, a grader since replaced). Stated with the reason.
  • Not measurable — the instrument cannot carry the question, and that is the result.

Publications

The Calibration, The Log, The Bench

The Calibration is the dated record of what was measured, written and signed by a person. The Log is what ran, what it cost and what moved, computed by the harness and assembled by the AI reporter; no person wrote it. The Bench is working notes and argument, written by a person, posted Sundays and Wednesdays. Issues ship on alternate Saturdays at 15:37 America/Chicago; an issued number is never edited.

The name

STAMP shares its name with several unrelated protocols, papers, and products — see Other STAMPs for the full list and how to cite this one unambiguously.

How to cite

By volume and date, which every issue carries in its name, with the URL, which is permanent. Write the full name, The STAMP Protocol, never “the STAMP protocol” alone, which in networking means RFC 8762; Other STAMPs spells out the distinction.
