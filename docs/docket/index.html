    2026-09-30
    California

    California: 13 AI bills signed, including human review of automated discipline and AI provenance

    Supportedlawsigned

    Signed 13 bills. Within scope: SB 947 requires a person to review automated employment disciplinary decisions; AB 2713 and SB 1000 amend the California AI Transparency Act on provenance data for AI-generated material; SB 1159 adds AI transparency and governance requirements. Others cover workplace surveillance, health care, legal practice and digital replicas.

    What it asks to be measured. Provenance data that lets AI-generated material be identified (AB 2713, SB 1000); human review of automated decisions in employment discipline (SB 947).

    “AI should expand opportunity – not come at the expense of workers and families.”Gavin Newsom, Governor of California, official press release

    Source: Office of the Governor of California. Checked 2026-10-05.

    Limits: Bill-level detail is the Governor's one-line summaries; individual bill texts not retrieved.

    2026-09-18
    California

    California Executive Order N-9-26: accelerating independent oversight

    Supportedexecutive orderin force

    Directs the Government Operations Agency, with the Office of Emergency Services, to speed up implementation of SB 813 and AB 1405 and to convene outside experts within two months. Measures under consideration include placing independent verification organizations onsite at frontier AI developers, verified safety frameworks, an emergency shutoff mechanism for frontier models, and counting loss-of-control events as critical safety incidents.

    What it asks to be measured. Regular onsite audits by independent verifiers; verification of developers' safety frameworks and risk assessments (under consideration, not yet required).

    Source: Office of the Governor of California · Office of the Governor of California. Checked 2026-10-05.

    Limits: Summarised from the release; the signed order's PDF was located but not read in full.

    2026-09-09
    California

    California SB 813: independent verification organizations

    Supportedlawsignedapplies 2028-01-01

    Signed and chaptered (Chapter 179). Creates a state framework under which the Government Operations Agency designates 'independent verification organizations' (IVOs): AI auditors with demonstrated expertise in assessing the risks of AI systems and models. The agency must set application requirements, qualification criteria and suspension procedures by 1 January 2028.

    What it asks to be measured. IVOs must identify the benchmarks, metrics and methodologies behind each assessment, stay independent of the party assessed (no payment conditioned on results), and file annual reports to the agency and the Legislature.

    Source: California Legislature · Office of the Governor of California. Checked 2026-10-05.

    2026-09-09
    California

    California AB 1405: state registry for AI auditors

    Supportedlawsigned

    Signed the same day as SB 813. Creates a state registry for AI auditors with independence and transparency standards.

    What it asks to be measured. Who may audit AI systems, and the independence and transparency standards an auditor must meet to be registered.

    Source: Office of the Governor of California. Checked 2026-10-05.

    Limits: Summarised from the Governor's release; the bill text itself was not retrieved for this entry.

    2026-07-27
    European Union

    EU Digital Omnibus on AI: high-risk obligations deferred

    Supportedregulationin forceapplies 2027-12-02

    The AI Omnibus amending the AI Act entered into force on 27 July 2026. High-risk obligations for stand-alone (Annex III) systems in areas such as biometrics, employment, education and critical infrastructure now apply from 2 December 2027; for AI embedded in regulated products (Annex I), from 2 August 2028. General-purpose AI model obligations have applied since 2 August 2025.

    What it asks to be measured. Unchanged in kind: risk management, testing, accuracy and robustness requirements for high-risk systems now bind later; GPAI evaluation duties already apply.

    Source: European Commission · EUR-Lex (Official Journal). Checked 2026-10-05.

    Limits: Dates from the Commission's page. The regulation number (2026/1744) and Official Journal date (24 July 2026) come from secondary sources; EUR-Lex did not render to our fetcher.

    2026-05-14
    Colorado

    Colorado SB26-189 repeals and replaces the Colorado AI Act

    Supportedlawsignedapplies 2027-01-01

    Signed 14 May 2026. Repeals and re-enacts the provisions of SB 24-205, the 2024 Colorado AI Act, around 'automated decision-making technology' used in consequential decisions such as employment, housing and financial services, in place of 'high-risk artificial intelligence systems'.

    What it asks to be measured. Disclosure and consumer rights around automated decisions; the impact-assessment model of the 2024 Act is replaced.

    Source: Colorado General Assembly. Checked 2026-10-05.

    Limits: The 1 January 2027 date for the new obligations comes from secondary sources; the bill page lists the act's effective date as 14 May 2026.

    2026-05-13
    United Kingdom

    UK King's Speech 2026: no standalone AI bill

    Likelystatementstatement

    The King's Speech did not announce a dedicated AI bill. It announced a Regulating for Growth Bill with cross-economy sandboxing powers that would let businesses test products, including AI, in controlled environments. Sector regulators keep their AI remits.

    What it asks to be measured. None stated for AI evaluation; sandbox testing powers proposed.

    Source: House of Commons Library · IAPP (secondary). Checked 2026-10-05.

    Limits: The Commons Library briefing refused our fetcher (403); summary rests on IAPP and law-firm reports that agree.

    2026-03-27
    New York

    New York RAISE Act chapter amendment

    Likelylawsignedapplies 2027-01-01

    A chapter amendment signed 27 March 2026 is reported to be the final form of the RAISE Act: it aligns key definitions with California's Transparency in Frontier AI Act while keeping a 72-hour incident-reporting window (California's is 15 days), with obligations from 1 January 2027.

    What it asks to be measured. As above; definitions aligned with California's frontier-model law.

    Source: Wiley Rein LLP (law firm) (secondary) · Cooley LLP (law firm) (secondary). Checked 2026-10-05.

    Limits: No primary source retrieved for the amendment; two law-firm analyses agree on the date and terms.

    2026-01-22
    South Korea

    South Korea AI Basic Act and Enforcement Decree take effect

    Likelylawin force

    The AI Basic Act and its Enforcement Decree took effect on 22 January 2026, combining support for the AI industry with baseline duties of trust, safety, transparency and user protection. The ministry (MSIT) is deferring fact-finding investigations and fines for at least a year except in exceptional cases.

    What it asks to be measured. Advance notice to users of high-impact and generative AI; labelling of synthetic content; risk management plans, explainability and human-oversight documentation for high-impact systems.

    Source: US International Trade Administration (secondary) · US Library of Congress, Global Legal Monitor (secondary). Checked 2026-10-05.

    Limits: Official US-government summaries, not the Korean issuer's text; the Library of Congress page refused our fetcher (403).

    2025-12-19
    New York

    New York RAISE Act signed (S6953B/A6453B)

    Supportedlawsignedapplies 2027-01-01

    Requires frontier AI developers to create and publish safety protocol information and to report critical-harm incidents to the state within 72 hours. Oversight sits with a new office in the Department of Financial Services, which assesses large frontier developers and issues annual transparency reports. Penalties up to $1 million for a first violation and $3 million for later ones.

    What it asks to be measured. Published safety protocols; incident reports within 72 hours; state assessment of large frontier developers.

    “setting a strong standard for frontier AI safety”Kathy Hochul, Governor of New York, official press release

    Source: Office of the Governor of New York. Checked 2026-10-05.

    Limits: The 1 January 2027 date is the amended law's, from the entry below.

    2025-12-11
    United States (federal)

    US Executive Order 14365: national policy framework and state AI laws

    Supportedexecutive orderin force

    Directs the Attorney General to form an AI Litigation Task Force to challenge state AI laws that conflict with federal policy; the Commerce Secretary to identify 'onerous' state laws within 90 days; agencies to consider conditioning BEAD and discretionary grants; the FCC to begin a proceeding on a federal AI reporting standard; and officials to draft preemptive federal legislation, with carve-outs including child safety.

    What it asks to be measured. A proposed federal AI reporting standard (FCC proceeding); otherwise none stated. Bears on whether state evaluation and audit laws, such as California's, remain enforceable.

    Source: The White House · US Government Publishing Office (Federal Register). Checked 2026-10-05.

    Limits: An executive order cannot itself overturn state law; that requires Congress or the courts.

    How an entry gets here

    The rules

    An event enters only if it requires, funds, restricts or defines the testing, evaluation, auditing, transparency or incident reporting of AI systems, or moves the date such a requirement applies. Each entry must pass a gate before this page is built: real dates, a primary source from the issuer, a retrieval record for anything labelled Supported, quotes of 15 words or fewer from an official record with the speaker named, no loaded words outside quotation marks, and a re-check within 60 days for anything that can still change. No party labels and no verdicts. An agent drafts entries; a person approves every one.
