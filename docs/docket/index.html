    Executive summary

    In brief

    What the 160 entries below show, as of Oct 09, 2026. Each point links to the entries it rests on.

    1. Binding AI law arrived, unevenly. The EU AI Act (in force Aug 01, 2024) is the first comprehensive AI law. South Korea's AI Basic Act took effect Jan 22, 2026, and national laws followed in Italy, Kazakhstan, Vietnam and Taiwan. Most countries still govern AI through strategies and guidance: the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Kenya, Colombia, Malaysia, Türkiye, Israel and Russia.
    2. Frontier-model transparency became state law in the US. California SB 53, New York's RAISE Act and Illinois SB 315 require large developers to publish safety frameworks and report critical incidents: within 15 days in California and 72 hours in New York. Illinois adds annual independent third-party audits.
    3. Independent evaluation is being built into institutions. National AI safety institutes now test models in the UK, the US, Japan, Canada and Australia, and published a joint pre-deployment evaluation in Nov 2024. California created independent verification organizations and an auditor registry in Sep 2026.
    4. US federal policy has swung between administrations. EO 14110 required red-team reporting for large models; it was revoked on Jan 20, 2025 and followed by EO 14179, EO 14365 on state AI laws, EO 14409 on voluntary frontier-model access, and EO 14434, which renamed AI 'Super Intelligence' after the President's UN address.
    5. Labelling AI-generated content is the most widely shared duty. China (from Sep 01, 2025), India (from Feb 20, 2026), the EU and California (both from Aug 02, 2026), Kazakhstan and Vietnam require AI-generated content to be marked or labelled.
    6. Deadlines keep moving. The EU Digital Omnibus deferred high-risk obligations to Dec 02, 2027 and Aug 02, 2028. Colorado delayed its 2024 AI Act and then replaced it in May 2026.
    7. The Global South is writing its own frameworks. The African Union strategy, the African Commission's resolution on meaningful human control, Latin America's Santiago and Montevideo declarations, Chile's AI bill and Brazil's set regional terms rather than adopting the EU model wholesale.
    8. Militaries write their own AI rules. Civil AI laws exclude defence, so armed forces govern AI through their own directives and declarations: the US autonomous-weapons directive, now being rewritten under NSPM-11 after an ‘AI-first’ memo; the UK's JSP 936; NATO's principles; and the non-binding Political Declaration and REAIM 2026, which the US and China did not endorse.
