    The Bench

    The blog · what we did not run ourselvesMondays and Thursdays · 07:37 CT

    What we read, watch and argue with. The Calibration is what we ran ourselves; The Log is what the machine reports. The Bench is everything else — other people’s data, other labs’ models, and the questions we are still working out in public. A position rather than a measurement: written by a person, who signs it, and open to being attacked in public. Nothing here is a finding; when something on this page turns out to be worth measuring, it goes into a Calibration.

    Up next, not yet published: Vol 007, due Oct 15, 2026, 07:37 America/Chicago. The two essays at the foot came before the Bench was numbered.

    Vol 006 · A Name Is Not a TestOct 12, 2026

    The US renamed AI ‘Super Intelligence’ without changing what the word covers, and the UN talks on autonomous weapons ended without a treaty. Both turn on one question: who checks what an AI is said to be? Why we still write “AI,” where military AI rules reach testing, and what we propose, starting with a public-test line on every Docket entry.

    Vol 005 · Judges Who Agree With Each OtherOct 08, 2026

    Our three judges agreed with each other at 0.875 and with one person at 0.631 to 0.685. Nine published studies of AI judges, 2023 to 2026, bear on that and none measured it: what each found, where it reaches our work, and what we propose to take from it, starting with printing four numbers for every judge instead of one.

    Vol 004 · What a New Judge Has to BeatOct 05, 2026

    Two decision models went through the seven rules of Vol 003. The agreement bar was met, to three decimal places, by a judge that only ever says PASS, and the rule that asked a person is what stopped them. A proposal for how a new kind of model, Jev or whatever comes next, should be tested here: seven steps, starting with the judge that always says yes.

    Vol 003 · Five Cases, a Hundred Times EachOct 01, 2026

    LangChain reported that Jev, a new decision model, matched a human reviewer on all 500 pass/fail decisions it judged. The 500 were five cases repeated a hundred times. What that can carry, the open models that followed, and the seven rules any judge must pass before it grades our work. Due Sep 14, 2026; published seventeen days late, and the post says why.

    Vol 002 · Something It Can Actually CheckSep 10, 2026

    Four Claude models were handed a record with two errors planted in it, twice — once with web search off, once with it on. All twelve cells caught both. Retrieval changed nothing about that, and everything about what could honestly be said afterwards. Run record · data.

    Vol 001 · The No. 1 ComplaintSep 06, 2026

    Is hallucination really what people hate most about AI? The review data says yes. Then thirteen AI models were asked the same question, word for word, and the answer that came back was that our own table was wrong. It was, nine times over. Run record · PDF · source.

    Essay · Stop marking it like a studentSep 02, 2026

    A model guesses when your question looks like it wants help, and hedges when it looks like it might cause harm. Neither behavior is about what the model knows. Both are about how it is marked.

    Essay · Why “I don’t know” can be a lieSep 02, 2026

    Most prompt hygiene guards against invention and stops there. The case for treating abstention as a symmetric failure — and what it costs to guard only one side.
