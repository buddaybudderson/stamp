    AI governance and history since 2020 · newest firstA Budday Budderson Production

    The wider record the Docket leaves out: court rulings, enforcement actions, speeches, votes and milestones in AI since 2020, worldwide. Each entry names its sources, says how sure we are with the labels a STAMP reply uses, and shows the date it was last checked. Instruments that change how AI is evaluated are in the Docket.

    Not legal advice. Summaries are short by design and may omit conditions that matter to you. Read the source. Supported means we opened the issuer’s own document. Likely means official or expert summaries agree but the issuer’s text was not retrieved. What we checked and declined to print is at the foot of the page.

    Executive summary

    In brief

    What the 47 entries below show, as of Oct 09, 2026. Each point links to the entries it rests on.

    1. Courts are drawing the copyright lines. Training on lawfully acquired books was held fair use in Bartz v. Anthropic, which then settled for USD 1.5 billion (final approval Jul 20, 2026); copying for a competing tool was not fair use in Thomson Reuters v. Ross. A UK court held model weights are not an infringing copy (Getty v Stability); a Munich court found memorised lyrics infringe (GEMA v OpenAI).
    2. Authorship and inventorship stay human. Courts in the UK and US, the US Copyright Office and the USPTO all required a human author or inventor.
    3. Privacy and consumer regulators acted first. Italy's data-protection authority limited ChatGPT in 2023, fined OpenAI EUR 15 million and blocked DeepSeek; Clearview AI was fined in the UK and the Netherlands; the FTC banned Rite Aid from facial recognition and pursued deceptive AI claims.
    4. Organisations answer for what their AI says. Air Canada was held to its chatbot's answer; lawyers were sanctioned for fake ChatGPT citations; and South Africa withdrew a national AI policy whose references were fictitious.
    5. Leaders framed AI as a question of governance. The UN Secretary-General addressed the Security Council, the Commission President proposed an IPCC-style panel, the US Vice President argued against excessive regulation in Paris, and Pope Leo XIV tied his papal name to AI. Speeches that announced evaluation bodies are in the Docket, such as the UK Prime Minister's of Oct 26, 2023.
    6. The technology moved faster than the rules. From GPT-3 (May 2020) to ChatGPT (Nov 30, 2022), GPT-4 and Claude (Mar 14, 2023), and open-weight DeepSeek-R1 (Jan 20, 2025); AI research won the Physics and Chemistry Nobel Prizes in 2024.

    How an entry gets here

    The rules

    An event enters if it is part of the public record of AI since 2020: a court ruling or filing, an enforcement action, a speech by a head of state or government or the leader of an international body, a legislative vote, or a technical or institutional milestone. Each entry must pass a gate before this page is built: real dates, a primary source from the issuer, a retrieval record for anything labelled Supported, quotes of 15 words or fewer from an official record with the speaker named, no loaded words outside quotation marks, and a re-check within 60 days for anything that can still change. Key claims (dates, numbers, holdings) were matched verbatim against the full text of the sources. No party labels and no verdicts. An agent drafts entries; a person approves every one.
