    On Sep 22, 2026 the President of the United States told the UN General Assembly that artificial intelligence would “hereinafter” be called “Super Intelligence.” A week later an executive order put the new name to work across the executive branch. This page records where the term came from, who has followed it, who has not, and how this site writes it.

    In brief

    A new label, the same legal definition

    What changed

    Executive Order 14434, Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence (signed Sep 29, 2026; Federal Register Oct 02, 2026, doc. 2026-20321), tells executive agencies to write “Super Intelligence” and “SI” where they wrote “Artificial Intelligence” and “AI,” in correspondence, communications, websites, reports and other non-statutory documents, “to the maximum extent permitted by law.” [1][2][24]

    What did not

    The terms carry the statutory definition of artificial intelligence in 15 U.S.C. 9401(3). Statutes, regulations, contracts, grants and documents already issued are not rewritten. A proposed federal definition is due from the Assistant to the President for Science and Technology within 60 days, around Nov 28, 2026. [1][25][26]

    Uses “SI”

    The federal executive branch, by order. The State Department told staff “We will be using ‘SI’ in all remarks, positions and press elements moving forward.” [9] NIST has said it is updating its communications. [30]

    Keeps “AI”, so far

    No major AI company has renamed a product; Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta, SpaceXAI and NVIDIA would not say whether they will. [10] No other government, and no major news stylebook, has been found adopting the term. California’s governor signed an order keeping “AI” for state agencies. [12][13]

    Why it is contested. “Superintelligence” already names a hypothetical system that exceeds human ability in nearly every field, which no one claims exists. “SI” already names the International System of Units. [11][12]
    This site. Protocol STAMP keeps writing “AI.” It quotes ‘Super Intelligence’ (SI) when it names or quotes a US instrument that uses it. Why.

    Timeline

    How the term moved

    1. 1998
      Philosopher Nick Bostrom writes about “superintelligence”: an intellect that greatly exceeds human cognitive performance in virtually all domains. This is the research meaning the word still carries.Source [12][35]
    2. Jun 03, 2025
      The US AI Safety Institute is reformed as the Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI), dropping “safety” from the name.Source Docket [29]
    3. 2025 / 2026
      Bills in Congress use “artificial superintelligence” in the research sense: Hawley and Blumenthal (2025, S. 2938) define it as AI that can “match or exceed human cognitive performance” across most domains; Sanders and Casar (2026) propose to ban it.Source [11][39][17][18]
    4. Sep 22, 2026
      At the UN General Assembly the President says the United States “totally rejects any attempt to construct a globalist scheme of control” for AI, “hereinafter officially called ‘Super Intelligence.’” He said he hoped the rest of the world would follow.Source Docket [3][6][8]
    5. Sep 23, 2026
      At a UN Security Council meeting on AI and international security, the US (OSTP Director Michael Kratsios) rejects “a globalist scheme of control for superintelligence” and “a global regulator,” and offers partners a “sovereign superintelligence capability.”Source [4][5]
    6. Sep 24, 2026
      Reports that diplomats were ordered to say “super intelligence”; a State Department email directs “SI” in all remarks, positions and press elements.Source [7][9]
    7. Sep 29, 2026
      EO 14434 signed. Same day: a voluntary frontier-model accord with six company leaders, and a companion order creating America.gov, described as “SI-powered.”Source Docket [1][19][24][27]
    8. Oct 02, 2026
      The order is published in the Federal Register.Source [2]
    9. Oct 01, 2026to Oct 07, 2026
      Fact-checkers and trade press examine the term. PolitiFact and Poynter report “little to no change” in how the industry speaks; Newsweek and Poynter report that Musk said SpaceXAI (formerly xAI) will become “SpaceXSI,” and The San Francisco Standard that Jensen Huang adopted the term in a post; Sam Altman keeps “AI.” None of these was confirmed on the companies’ own channels.Source [11][12][13][15]
    10. Oct 08, 2026
      The President calls anyone who says “Artificial Intelligence” instead of “Super Intelligence” “THE ENEMY!” Axios notes the White House itself still uses “AI” in places. The NIST page for CAISI reads “Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence (CAISSI)”; the instrument for that rename was not found.Source [14][20][21] Docket
    11. Nov 28, 2026about; due
      Proposed federal definition of the term (60 days from the order). If Congress adopts one, the legal meaning may change; until then it does not.Source [1][25]

    Two meanings, one word

    Why the term is contested

    The research meaning

    In books, lab charters and bills, “superintelligence” (or ASI, artificial superintelligence) means a system that exceeds human cognition in nearly all fields. Google DeepMind’s levels-of-AGI ranking puts it at the top, “not yet achieved.” [11][12]

    The federal meaning

    Under EO 14434, “Super Intelligence” means whatever 15 U.S.C. 9401(3) calls artificial intelligence: today’s systems, including ordinary ones. [1]

    The clash. A federal document that says “SI” and a bill that says “artificial superintelligence” can now be about different things. Experts quoted by Poynter warn this could complicate regulation. In science and engineering, “SI” is also the International System of Units. [12][31]
    “It sounds much better. It is much better, and it’s much more accurate.”The President, at the UN General Assembly, Sep 22, 2026, on the new name; he added that “artificial” makes it “sound fake.” [38]

    House style

    How Protocol STAMP writes it

    For adopting “SI”

    • Federal documents will increasingly say SI; matching them keeps citations and searches aligned with primary sources.
    • Refusing it outright could look like taking a side.
    • If Congress writes it into law, the switch is already made.

    Against

    • It reads as a capability claim (“super”) that an evaluation program exists to test, not to assume.
    • “SI” collides with SI units and with the research term ASI.
    • The legal definition is unchanged, so the new label adds no meaning.
    • The archive, every other government, every standard and every lab still say “AI.” A partial switch splits the record.
    The rule, for now. We write “AI.” When we name or quote a US instrument that uses the new term, we quote it: ‘Super Intelligence’ (SI), with a note on first use that it is the US federal term for AI under EO 14434 and carries the same legal definition. We do not write a bare “SI” in our own voice. We will revisit the rule when the proposed federal definition is published; any change will be logged in Corrections + changes.
