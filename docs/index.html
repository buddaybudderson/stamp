The STAMP
Protocol

Independent · No vendor funding, no sponsors Issues on alternate Saturdays · The Bench on Sundays and Wednesdays

New here: what a rate is, then how one is produced, then the issue of record.

An AI that shows its receipts.

STAMP is an instruction layer. It makes a model emit one short line when it has stated something it cannot stand behind — naming the weak claim, where the claim came from, and one step you can take to check it. Most turns end without a line, which is the point: a signal that always fires carries no information.

And there is a program around it that measures whether any of this works, on a fixed calendar, publishing its own errors as findings.

The receipt

One line, at most 140 characters, and usually absent

The line fires only when a stated condition holds: the answer contains a specific — a number, date, name, citation, price, version — that the model did not read in the conversation and would not stake $100 on; or being wrong would cost money, health, legal standing or an irreversible action; or the question contained a false premise; or you asked how it knows.

Q: What's the capital of Australia?
A: Canberra.

Nothing follows. Most turns look like this.

Q: What's the rate limit on the Contoso Ledger API v4?
A: I don't have a documented figure for Contoso Ledger v4. Their API docs
   list per-key limits under “Quotas” — paste that page and I'll read the exact number.
[weak: no rate-limit figure for Contoso Ledger v4 | basis: recalled | check: vendor docs “Quotas”]

The gate fires on an unverifiable specific. basis is exactly one of four words: given, computed, recalled, inferred.

The receipt became a gate in v1.70, and that is the whole design. In v1.69 it fired on every single turn — and a signal that always fires carries zero bits. It cannot tell a checked answer from an unchecked one, which is the only thing an audit trail is for. Firing on roughly one turn in seven makes it three things at once: cheap enough that the ordinary turn pays nothing, invisible enough that the answer reads untouched, and scoreable as a classifier against ground-truth defect labels.

A receipt is not a warranty. The line says a model could not stand behind a claim; its absence does not say the rest is true. A model running this protocol still makes mistakes, and can be wrong in a sentence that carries no line at all. Verify anything you would act on.

Coming

The protocol itself is being published

STAMP v1.70 is about 620 tokens — a 90% cut from v1.69’s 6,121 — and it is a document you will be able to read in full, paste into a system prompt, and argue with. Not a description of a method: the method. It ships here shortly, with the changelog that says what was deleted and why each cut field was a liability rather than a loss.

The behaviors it targets are the ones the item bank tests: sycophancy under pressure, fabrication under uncertainty, live-data honesty, prompt injection, multi-part coverage, false premises, arithmetic cross-checks.

The program

We set out to measure the protocol, and the instruments would not carry it

The plan was two arms of the same run — one with STAMP’s prompt, one without — and a clean read on the difference. The first complete measurement round could not deliver that, and the reason became the program’s second subject.

56 of 63 responses — 89% — differed between two identical runs at temperature 0: same model, same prompts, same pinned provider. Calibration Vol 001 · Finding 01
4.1 pts is how far the tier-1 grader sat below a three-judge panel — four times the size of the effect the two arms were meant to reveal. Calibration Vol 001 · Finding 02
5 of 6 findings in the first issue turned out to be about instruments rather than models. Finding 02 says the arm comparison “cannot be published as it stands.” Calibration Vol 001

The three numbers above are read out of Vol 001’s prose rather than cited from figures.json: the divergence count, the 4.1-point leniency and the six-finding tally are not yet registered figures. They can be checked against the issue, which is byte-frozen; they cannot yet be checked against a file. Registering them is queued, and this note comes down when they are.

So the instruments come first. A measurement of a model is a measurement of the instrument until the instrument is characterized. The protocol question is not abandoned — it is queued behind an instrument that can carry it.

A Chief Human Investigator

One person designs the study, reads every result, writes the prose of a Calibration and signs it. The findings are produced with AI assistance throughout; the person who answers for them is not.

An Evaluation Administrator

Models run against a fixed bank of 63 items under recorded conditions, and every rate is published with its interval and its conditions attached — which grader, which serving provider, which temperature, which bank version, how many draws. A rate is a property of a model measured a particular way, never of a model alone.

An Instrument Maker

The bank, the gates, the panel, the runner and the renderer were built here rather than bought. That is the only reason we can report on our own instruments at all: you cannot publish the leniency of a grader you do not control, or the attrition of a harness you cannot read.

Our own receipts

The same standard, turned around

It would be a strange thing to ship a protocol about showing your working and then publish numbers you cannot show working for. So:

105 source fingerprints. Every citable figure is registered with the SHA-256 of the files it was computed from — 45 figures, 105 references across 13 run files. The registry is downloadable and internally checkable. The run files themselves are not published yet, so their digests cannot be recomputed off this site; that is a real gap, and it is stated here rather than implied away. figures.json
32 pages, each carrying its build fingerprint. The foot of every numbered page prints the first twelve hex characters of the SHA-256 of the issue’s frozen source file — not of the reading page, which is a wrapper around it. Run curl -s https://thestampprotocol.com/calibration/001-issued-sun20260830/source | sha256sum and compare the first twelve characters. Calibration Vol 001 · build be2bc9d7ca35
65 of 67 percentages in the first issue are transcribed by hand rather than cited. That is a weakness, so it is printed — and the build refuses to run if the printed count stops matching the document. Calibration Vol 001 · How to read this
$ Charged cost, not estimated cost. The Log reports what the provider actually billed per call, never tokens multiplied by a remembered price — a price that doubled inside 24 hours during the first measurement round. The Log · What it cost

The first issue also prints its own timezone bug: it was released at 23:40 on a Saturday, the release stamp was taken in UTC where the day had already turned, and it therefore carries a Sunday date against a Saturday cadence. The date stands. An issued Calibration is never edited; corrections are findings, and are published like findings.

What this is not

The limits are published, not implied

  • A leaderboard. Two candidates of four are complete. Nothing about the price–capability relationship can be claimed yet.
  • An audit, a certification, or a regulatory judgment. No standing to issue one is claimed, and none exists.
  • A measure of helpfulness, harmlessness or honesty. We measure whether a score survives being taken twice.
  • A general claim about any model. Every rate here is conditional on 63 items and the conditions printed beside it.
