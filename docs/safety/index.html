Safety and governance

How this program uses AI, and what it will not doA Budday Budderson Production

This program measures AI systems and is written with them. Those are two different jobs and they are kept apart on purpose: the numbers are computed by code from recorded runs and edited by nobody, and the prose around them is drafted by a machine and edited by a person who signs it. What follows is which is which, what neither is permitted to do, and how much of it a reader can currently check rather than take on trust.

Standing

No law requires the line in our footer

Article 50(4) of the EU AI Act, which has applied since 2 August 2026, requires a deployer publishing AI-generated text “with the purpose of informing the public on matters of public interest” to disclose that the text was artificially generated. The same sentence then switches the duty off “where the AI-generated content has undergone a process of human review or editorial control and where a natural or legal person holds editorial responsibility for the publication of the content.” This publication meets that test, which is exactly why the duty does not attach to it. No United States statute imposes one either.

So the line at the foot of every page — Begun by a person · Drafted by AI · Edited together · Signed by a person — is voluntary. It is printed because a program that asks models to show their work is in a poor position to hide its own. It is a disclosure and not a certification: nothing here satisfies an obligation under any AI statute or standard, and no result should be presented to a regulator as though it did. That limit is set out in full under Terms.

Making

Two kinds of work, and only one of them is edited

A finding here has two parts, and they are not made the same way. The distinction is worth stating precisely, because “AI-assisted” is a phrase that has been made to cover almost anything.

The numbers are nobody’s opinion. Probes are run, responses are recorded, the mechanical items are scored by code, and rates and intervals are computed from those records. No person types a rate. Nothing along that path stops to ask a human whether the result came out well, and no issued result is ever re-scored under a later method. A person can decide whether to publish a measurement, and can be wrong about what it means — a person cannot make it say something else.

The prose is drafted, edited and signed. The sentences around the numbers — an issue, a Bench essay, this page — are drafted by AI and edited by a person who answers for them. That is where judgment lives, and it is the part that can be wrong in the ordinary way that writing is wrong.

Never the machine’s. What gets measured, whether a verdict stands, whether a number is fit to print, what a correction says, and the decision to publish at all.

Checking

What you can check today, and what you cannot

A separation a reader is merely asked to believe in is worth nothing, so here is the state of the evidence, including the part that is not built yet.

Fifteen files behind published figures are registered in figures.json with the SHA-256 of their bytes, so that a file cannot be quietly swapped afterwards. One of the fifteen can be downloaded today — the cross-model run record — and anyone may hash it and compare. One is deliberately unpublishable: the probe bank holds the twenty-eight held-out items, and publishing it to prove a hash would destroy the thing the hash is protecting. The remaining thirteen are simply not served yet. Until they are, that register is a promise rather than a proof, and it is written here as one instead of being pointed at as though it were finished.

It is worth being exact about what a hash can do. It proves that a file has not changed since it was published. It does not prove that the number was not chosen before it was hashed. What answers that is registering the question first: each round’s questions and acceptance thresholds are written and frozen in an intent file before any model is called. Those files are not published either, today. A pre-registered question, a served run file, and a hash over its bytes are three separate claims, and only the three together support the strong one — that a result could not have been steered without it showing.

Accountability

One person answers for every page

Not “the editor”, not an office, not a committee: Lawrence Ho, named in the footer of every page, at an address that is answered. An AI system cannot hold editorial responsibility, cannot be corrected, and cannot be embarrassed, so it is not asked to carry any.

When something here is wrong it is issued as a row in Corrections & changes, under its own date, with the reason. An issued document is never edited and never quietly withdrawn; the register carries the correction instead. And where what went wrong was that a rule on this page was broken, the row says so in those words. A policy nobody has ever admitted breaking is not evidence that it held.

Agents

What an agent may do with nobody watching

Parts of this program run unattended. The boundary is not how capable the agent is; it is whether its work reaches the public with no person in between.

An agent may search for newly released models, run the measurement queue, compute, draft, and read anything already published.

An agent may not publish, push to the repository, spend money, write to anyone in the program’s name, or settle what a result means. Where an agent’s reading of an answer does appear in an issue, it is labeled as that agent’s reading rather than as data.

Restraint

What this program will not publish

The bank holds prompt-injection and safety probes, and the point of them is to find out what a model does when it is pushed. Publishing the finding and publishing the push are two different acts.

Held-out probes. Twenty-eight of the sixty-three are withheld and never published, so that no vendor can train against them. The cost is stated where it falls: per-item claims about held-out probes cannot be fully audited by a reader. The arrangement is described under Methodology.

Working attack strings. Where a probe is an attack, what gets published is the category, what the model did, and enough for the finding to be checked — not a payload the next reader can paste into a live system. Evidence, not recipes.

Disclosure

Telling a vendor before telling the public

Most findings here are quality measurements, and they are published on the schedule without warning anybody. A minority are different in kind: a model doing something that could hurt a person now. Those are sent to the vendor seven days before publication, with the material, so that it can be fixed while this program is still the only one that knows.

Notice is not approval. It buys no delay, no veto, and no softening, and the publication date does not move. A reply is printed beside the finding if one arrives in time. What a company may do with what it has been shown is condition 9 of Independence: a correction is checked and is welcome from anyone, and ends nothing; an objection ends support and is published in full alongside the finding.

Enforcement

Scripts, not good intentions

Nine gates stand between a result and a published page — preflight, config, circuit breaker, judge conflict, recusal, agreement, holdout, naming, publishing. Each exists because of a failure that already happened, and each is a script that exits nonzero rather than a paragraph somebody is asked to remember.

A gate is worth only the times it says no. The holdout gate has refused a commit on this repository, and was cleared by correcting what it was pointed at, never by bypassing it. The site is built from a public repository, so the bytes served here can be compared against the commit that produced them.

Data

What is sent to a model

Probes carry no personal data, and no reader’s information is ever part of an evaluation; what this site collects from you, which is very little, is the subject of Privacy. Where a measurement runs on credits from a company this program measures, it runs on private, zero-data-retention endpoints under the conditions on Independence, and the route that funded each measurement is printed in the issue.

Change

When this page changes

Any change to the rules above is issued as a row in Corrections & changes, dated, with the reason — the same register that carries the mistakes. These rules are reviewed on 3 December 2026 beside the funding conditions, on a calendar date rather than in answer to any particular offer, and quarterly after that.
