About this publication

What a Calibration is

A Calibration is one dated issue of a measurement programme. It is not a leaderboard, and the thing it is mostly about is not the models — it is the instruments people use to rank them.

Vol 001 and Vol 002 asked whether the judges agree with each other. They do. This issue asks the question both of them left open: whether the judges agree with a person.

Bottom line

On these draws, our judges agree with each other more than any of them agrees with a person. The three seated judges agreed with one another at 0.875 [0.789, 0.950] (the same labelled draws), and with one person’s blind labels at 0.685 [0.515, 0.819] (labelled draws) (Gemini), 0.659 [0.479, 0.798] (labelled draws) (the tier-1 grader behind Vol 001) and 0.631 [0.453, 0.780] (labelled draws) (Grok). The intervals are wide and overlap, so this is a direction, not a measured gap.

Read every agreement figure here against one number. The person passed 77 of 87 draws. On a sample like that, a judge that answered PASS to everything would score 0.871 (labelled draws) — higher than every judge we use.

The judges fail more than the person does. The person failed 10 draws; each seated judge failed more than twice that many, most of them answers the person passed. They also missed some of the person’s failures. On Luna’s public half, then, the judges’ standard is stricter than this person’s. One labeller cannot say which standard is right.

The decision models agree with the person more because they fail almost nothing. Jev caught 3 of the person’s 10 failures and Laya 0. Neither meets rule three, so neither is eligible for a panel seat.

What was asked, and how

The labels. Lawrence Ho labelled all 87 public-half draws of the Luna panel sample from Vol 002, by each item’s own pass criterion, blind on the page to every judge: 77 PASS, 10 FAIL and 0 unsure, between 12:01 and 12:43 on 3 October, before the 18:00 freeze. A label could not be changed once saved.

The judges. Every judge’s verdict on these draws was already on disk from Vol 002: J-A google/gemini-3.7-flash, J-B x-ai/grok-4.6, J-C bytedance-seed/seed-2-1-turbo (the tier-1 grader), Jev typesafe/jev-1.13 and Laya convaiinnovations/laya. No model was called for this issue and nothing was spent.

The questions. Q1: each judge’s agreement with the person, as Gwet’s AC1 with a bootstrap interval. Q2: of the person’s FAILs, how many each judge caught; of the person’s PASSes, how many each judge failed. Q3: a decision model meets rule three if its agreement is no lower than the lowest seated judge’s and it catches at least half of the person’s FAILs.

Exploratory, on the same draws. The seated judges with one another: all three 0.875 [0.789, 0.950] (the same labelled draws); Gemini and Grok 0.905 [0.815, 0.981] (the same labelled draws); Gemini and the tier-1 grader 0.852 [0.737, 0.949] (the same labelled draws); Grok and the tier-1 grader 0.869 [0.763, 0.963] (the same labelled draws). A judge answering PASS to every draw would score 0.871 (labelled draws) against the person.

So two things are true at once. The judges agree with each other better than with the person, though the intervals overlap. And on a sample where the person passed most answers, a high agreement figure is easy to reach by passing everything; it is the failures that separate the judges.

No floor was pre-registered for this question. The comparisons above are description, not a test.

Each seated judge failed more of the person’s PASSes than the person failed draws in total, and each also missed some of the person’s FAILs. In counts, the disagreement runs mostly one way: the judges fail answers the person passes. As shares of each pile, the misses on the person’s few FAILs are proportionally larger, so the judges are not simply a harsher copy of the person; they are stricter overall and also disagree about which answers fail.

What it would mean, on this sample. The failure rate Vol 001 published for Luna was set by the tier-1 grader. If this person’s reading is the standard, then on these sampled draws from Luna’s public half that grader fails more than a person would. The sample is not random: it was stratified on the tier-1 verdict and topped up with contested items. Vol 001’s other headline rates, chiefly Nemotron’s, have no human labels, so nothing here says how they would move.
03Pre-registered · answers Vol 002’s open question

Neither decision model meets rule three

The pre-registered test had two parts. On agreement, both pass: the lowest seated judge scored 0.631 (labelled draws), and Jev and Laya each scored higher — as would a judge that passes everything, at 0.871 (labelled draws). On failures, both fail: Jev caught 3 of 10 and Laya 0 of 10, where at least half was required.

The person’s FAILs fall on only 6 items, up to 4 on one, so the failure half of this test rests on a handful of items.

Consequence. Vol 002 left the seating of a decision model to the investigator. Neither model is eligible under the rule as signed, and neither is seated.

One person

  • A reference, not ground truth. One labeller, who built the bank and had seen some of these items before.
  • What he already knew. Earlier the same day he signed Vol 002, which lists by item and draw the eight draws where Jev and Laya differ; all eight of those draws are among the ones he labelled here. The page did not show which draw was which, but the probe text identifies an item to the person who built the bank, so the prior exposure is real.
  • The pace. All 87 labels took 42 minutes; the median time between labels was 11.000 seconds, and 10 were saved under five seconds apart. Most responses are short, but this is quick, and a reader should weigh it.

Choices and departures, stated

  • The analysis code was written after labelling. scripts/cal003_figures.py was written by the drafting agent in one pass once the labels were complete, and before any figure was computed. It implements the signed definitions; the exploratory figures were added after the independent review and are labelled as such.
  • Public half only. Held-out draws were not labelled, so every labelled probe and response can be published. The labels are served at /data/runs/ with this issue.
  • The reference needs two judges to agree. As in Vol 002, draws where J-B and J-C disagree have no reference and are counted.
  • The labeller saw the stored response. On 2 draws the stored response is shorter than the text the tier-1 grader originally graded.
  • Nothing was run. Every verdict compared here was recorded for Vol 002; their run files are already served.

Standing limitations

  • One labeller. A second, independent labeller is the next step.
  • One model, one half. Luna, public half, 87 draws from 35 items.
  • Intervals are optimistic. The bootstrap treats draws as independent; draws within an item are not.
  • Data terms for the seated judges are still unchecked, as Vol 002 recorded.
