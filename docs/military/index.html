    Militaries and AI

    Civil AI laws stop at the barracks gate: the EU AI Act and the Council of Europe’s AI convention both exclude military and national-defence use. What governs AI in armed forces is a patchwork of national directives, alliance principles, non-binding declarations and a decade-old UN negotiation that has not produced a treaty. This page maps it.

    In brief

    Five things to know

    01 · Treaty

    No treaty yet

    The UN Secretary-General and the ICRC asked states in 2023 to conclude a binding instrument on autonomous weapons by 2026. The CCW expert group’s final session ended in the early hours of Sep 05, 2026 with an adopted, non-binding final report and no treaty; the text goes to the CCW Review Conference in November 2026. [1][2][3][64]

    02 · Votes

    Broad votes, narrow commitments

    164 states voted for the 2025 UN General Assembly resolution on lethal autonomous weapons (Res. 80/57; 156 had backed it in committee). But only 35 of 85 states at the 2026 REAIM summit signed its declaration at the time (organisers later said more than 40), and the US and China both stayed out. [65][5][9][10]

    03 · Control

    “Human control” is the shared word, not a shared rule

    Nearly every text says humans stay responsible; they differ on how much control, and whether existing law is enough. The US, Russia and India prefer non-binding or consensus outcomes; the African Group and 47 states led by Brazil call the elements text a basis for negotiation. [3][4][6]

    04 · United States

    The US is accelerating

    A Jan 09, 2026 memo made the department “AI-first” and required “any lawful use” terms in AI contracts; NSPM-11 (Jun 05, 2026) ordered DoD Directive 3000.09 on autonomous weapons rewritten; a Sep 30, 2026 memo plans an Autonomous Warfare Command by Oct 01, 2027. [23][26][29]

    05 · Vendors

    Vendors are now part of the governance

    When Anthropic refused to drop limits on fully autonomous weapons and domestic mass surveillance, the Pentagon designated it a supply-chain risk. Courts have split. In 2025, Microsoft cut an Israeli military unit’s access to some cloud and AI services. [24][25][53]

    Disclosure. Anthropic makes Claude, a tool this site uses and a vendor whose models this program measures. The entries on its dispute are reported here like any other, from the sources cited; see Independence.

    Global and regional

    The rules above the nations

    Red: binding, but military use is carved out. Amber: still being negotiated. Grey: not binding.

    CCW Group of Governmental Experts on lethal autonomous weapons (since 2017)Talks · no treatyWorks by consensus on a “rolling text” of elements for an instrument, first tabled Jul 2024. Sessions Mar 02–06 and Aug 31–Sep 04, 2026; the last ended in the early hours of Sep 05 with an adopted, non-binding final report. Report to the Seventh Review Conference, Nov 2026. More than 70 states support the text as a basis for negotiation. [2][3][4][6][64]
    UN General Assembly resolutions on autonomous weaponsNon-binding78/241 (Dec 2023), 79/62 (Dec 2024) and 80/57 (Dec 01, 2025), the third in a row. 80/57 urges the CCW to complete the elements “with a view to future negotiation”; it passed 164–6–7 in the General Assembly, after 156 votes for it in the First Committee. [65][5][7]
    UN Secretary-General and ICRC joint appeal (Oct 2023)AppealCalls for prohibitions and restrictions in a binding treaty concluded by 2026; repeated by the Secretary-General in 2025. [1][7]
    REAIM summits (Responsible AI in the Military Domain)Non-bindingThe Hague 2023 (call to action); Seoul 2024 (Blueprint for Action, about 60 endorsers; China did not sign); A Coruña, Spain, Feb 2026 (“Pathways to Action,” 20 principles; 35 of 85 attendees at signing, organisers later said 40+; the US and China opted out). [9][10][11]
    US Political Declaration on Responsible Military Use of AI and Autonomy (2023)Non-bindingAnnounced Feb 2023; 31 endorsers by Nov 2023, 53 by Mar 2024. China, Russia, India, Israel, Brazil and Saudi Arabia had not endorsed as of 2024. No 2026 count found. [12][13]
    US–China leaders’ statement, Lima, Nov 16, 2024Non-bindingBiden and Xi affirm “the need to maintain human control over the decision to use nuclear weapons.” First such statement by China; narrow. [14][15][16]
    NATO AI Strategy (Oct 2021; revised Jul 10, 2024)Alliance principlesSix Principles of Responsible Use: lawfulness, responsibility and accountability, explainability and traceability, reliability, governability, bias mitigation. The 2024 revision adds AI-enabled disinformation as a concern. [17][18]
    EU AI Act, Art. 2(3)Law · excludes militaryDoes not apply to AI used “exclusively for military, defence or national security purposes,” grounded in Art. 4(2) TEU. Dual-use systems placed on the civil market are still covered. [19][20]
    Council of Europe Framework Convention on AI (opened Sep 05, 2024)Treaty · excludes defenceExcludes national defence and limits coverage of national-security activity (Art. 3). Signed by the EU, the US and the UK on the first day. [21][22]

    How many states sign on

    Country by country

    How each military handles AI

    United States

    1. Jan 2023
      DoD Directive 3000.09 updated: senior review and testing before an autonomous weapon is fielded.Source [26]
    2. Jan 09, 2026
      The Secretary directs an “AI-first” force, “any lawful use” contract terms within 180 days, and reorganises the AI and technology hubs.Source [23]
    3. Feb 27, 2026
      The President directs agencies to stop using Anthropic’s models and the Secretary designates the company a supply-chain risk (letters dated Mar 03).Source [24]
    4. Mar 26, 2026
      A federal judge in California preliminarily enjoins the designation.Source [35]
    5. Jun 05, 2026
      NSPM-11, Artificial Intelligence in the National Security Enterprise, replaces the 2024 national security memorandum (NSM-25), orders 3000.09 rewritten within 90 days, and bars vendors from disabling, degrading or modifying a system warfighters depend on without government approval. Senators questioned the pace.Source [31][32][36][27][28]
    6. Aug 27, 2026
      The same California court vacates the designation as retaliation in violation of the First Amendment.Source [25][34]
    7. Sep 25, 2026
      The D.C. Circuit, 2–1, upholds the designation. Courts have split.Source [25]
    8. Sep 30, 2026
      A memo plans a four-star Autonomous Warfare Command.Source [29]
    9. Oct 01, 2027
      Target date for the command.Source [29]
    • Oversight. The FY2026 NDAA (Sec. 1061, now 10 U.S.C. 130g) requires notice to Congress within 30 days of any waiver of 3000.09. The Congressional Research Service notes no frontier model is publicly known to be used in US autonomous weapons. [30][66][33]
    • Diplomacy. Sponsor of the 2023 Political Declaration (no 2026 statement on it found); signed the 2024 Seoul Blueprint; declined the 2026 A Coruña declaration; at the UN in Sep 2026, rejected “a globalist scheme of control” for AI. [10][12] SI page

    China

    • Doctrine aims at “intelligentized” warfare (智能化), the stage after “informatized”; the 2017 national AI plan calls for AI in command decision-making and equipment. No public PLA policy on military AI ethics has been found. [38][39]
    • Submitted position papers on military AI to the CCW (2018, 2021). Has proposed separating “unacceptable” from “acceptable” autonomous weapons, a position analysts call “human oversight with Chinese characteristics.” [8][40]
    • Agreed human control over nuclear-use decisions with the US (2024); did not sign the Seoul Blueprint (2024) or the A Coruña declaration (2026). [9][14]

    Russia

    • Announced a Concept for developing and using AI-based weapons in its Armed Forces in 2022. Holds that international humanitarian law applies fully and needs no new instrument; wants the issue kept in the CCW, not moved to the UN General Assembly. [41][42][43]
    • Held military-AI consultations with China in Feb 2024 and reported aligned positions on the CCW process. [44]

    United Kingdom

    • Defence AI Strategy and the “Ambitious, Safe, Responsible” policy (Jun 2022) created the Defence AI Centre. JSP 936, Dependable AI in Defence (Nov 2024), is the binding directive: governance, development and assurance across the AI lifecycle, with a Responsible AI Senior Officer in each component. [45][46][47][48]
    • Strategic Defence Review (Jun 02, 2025): AI and software for a “10x more lethal” Army, land drone swarms, at least 10% of equipment procurement on new technology each year. [49]

    France

    • Created the Ministerial Agency for Defence AI (AMIAD) on May 01, 2024. Airbus agreed a €50 million contract (Dec 10, 2025) to put AMIAD-developed AI into French weapons and information systems, starting with the Spationav maritime-surveillance system. [50][51]
    • Opposes treating lethal autonomous weapons as the sole focus of AI talks and has rejected immediate negotiations on them alone. [52]

    Israel

    • Reporting since 2024 describes AI target-recommendation systems (“The Gospel,” “Lavender”) used in Gaza. An AP investigation reported Azure-hosted surveillance data cross-checked with in-house AI for targeting. In Sep 2025 Microsoft found evidence supporting elements of the reporting and disabled some services to a unit of Israel’s Ministry of Defence. [53][54][55]
    • Had not endorsed the US Political Declaration as of 2024. [13]

    Ukraine

    • The most extensive battlefield use. Drones lock onto a target and fly the last few hundred metres autonomously; the Brave1 cluster has backed 200+ AI projects. Analysts found no use of fully autonomous AI drones at scale as of mid-2025, and human-in-the-loop remains the norm. [56][57][58]

    Others

    • India. Defence AI Council and Defence AI Project Agency (2019). Did not sign the 2024 Seoul Blueprint; had not endorsed the US Political Declaration as of 2024. [13][59][60]
    • Japan, Australia, South Korea. Signed the Seoul Blueprint; South Korea hosted the 2024 summit. A 2024 trilateral study proposes a shared AI capability framework with the US. Australia relies on a technical method (MEAID) rather than a defence AI law. [60][61]
    • Netherlands. Hosted the first REAIM summit (2023); its disarmament ambassador chaired the CCW expert group in 2024. [6][11]
    • Germany. Works with the defence-AI firm Helsing to upgrade existing platforms; no 2025–2026 national position paper was found. [62]
    • Canada. Published a Department of National Defence and Armed Forces AI strategy. [63]

    What it asks to be measured

    Where testing enters

    Most of these texts turn on testing: 3000.09 requires verification, validation, test and evaluation before fielding; JSP 936 requires assurance across the lifecycle; NATO lists reliability and traceability as principles; NSPM-11 makes “assurance” one of four pillars. None publishes a common test, a pass mark, or how many systems were reviewed. The DoD has not reported how many autonomous weapons it has reviewed under 3000.09. [26][27][32][17][47][37]

    InstrumentRequires testing or assurancePublishes a common testPublishes a pass markReports how many systems were reviewed
    DoD Directive 3000.09YesNoNoNo
    UK JSP 936YesNoNoNo
    NATO Principles of Responsible UseYesNoNoNo
    NSPM-11YesNoNoNo
    Our reading, not a finding. The open question for evaluators is the same as in civil AI: who checks, against what, and who sees the result. On the public record, our reading is that in defence the answer is mostly the ministry, against its own criteria, in private. That is a judgement, not a finding.
