## Before submitting to arXiv

1. **Read every number against its source.** Each figure in Sections 5 and 6 names the Calibration
   issue it comes from. Open the issue and check it. Nothing in the report should be taken from memory.
2. **Open every reference.** Confirm authors, venue and identifier for all 16. Four ([9] to [12])
   were checked online on Oct 13, 2026; the other twelve were not.
3. **Decide on Section 6.** You can submit now as a methods and measurement report (the effect is
   stated as not established), or wait for the System 1 / System 2 study and add its results. The first
   is honest and citable now. The second is a stronger paper.
4. **Make the release citable.** Tag a release on GitHub, connect the repository to Zenodo, and put the
   DOI in the Reproducibility section.
5. **Get an endorsement.** First-time submitters to an arXiv category need endorsement from an
   established author in that category. Suggested primary category: cs.CL; cross-list cs.AI and cs.CR.
   Ask a researcher whose work you cite or who has replicated your harness. Do not pay for an
   endorsement or use a service that sells them.
6. **Authorship and AI use.** arXiv does not accept AI systems as authors; the author takes
   responsibility for all content. The report already discloses AI use. Keep that section.
7. **Licence.** Choose CC BY 4.0 on the arXiv form so others can reuse figures with credit.

## After arXiv

- Submit to a peer-reviewed venue. Good fits: workshops on LLM evaluation, trustworthy or safe ML, or
  prompt injection at NeurIPS, ICLR, ACL or EMNLP, and USENIX or IEEE S&P workshops for the injection
  findings. Check each venue's dual-submission rules against the arXiv preprint.
- Invite replication: open an issue template "Replication report" and link it from the README.
